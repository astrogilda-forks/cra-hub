# Where do the CRA and the AI Act meet?

A product with digital elements that is also, or contains, an AI system falls under both the CRA and the [AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj). The two regulations ask for overlapping things at six points. For each one, the table gives the provision on each side, the evidence each requires, and who can check that evidence.

| Meeting point | CRA, on the product with digital elements | AI Act, on the AI system or model | Evidence each requires | Who can check it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cybersecurity of the system | Annex I Part I (1) and (2); Part II processes. Art 12(1) deems a high-risk AI system that is a PDE to comply with AI Act Art 15 cybersecurity where Parts I and II are met and the EU DoC demonstrates it | Art 15(5), resilience against unauthorised alteration of use, outputs or performance | Cybersecurity risk assessment in the technical documentation (Art 13(4), Art 31, Annex VII); the EU DoC | Notified body, via CRA Art 12(2) reading across to AI Act Art 43; MSA on request. The bridge discharges Art 15 cybersecurity only, not the rest of Art 15 |
| Recording what the system did | Annex I Part I (2)(l), provide security related information by recording and monitoring relevant internal activity | Art 12(1) and (2) logging over the lifetime; Art 19(1) provider retention; Art 26(6) deployer retention, min. six months; Art 21(2) access on reasoned request | The logs themselves | **Nobody independently.** Arts 19(1) and 21(2) both say "to the extent such logs are under their control" and neither asks the holder to evidence that control. A faithful record and a revised one reach the authority looking the same |
| Third-party components | Art 13(5) due diligence; Annex I Part II (1), SBOM covering at least top-level dependencies | Art 25(1) value-chain re-designation; Art 53(1)(b) and Annex XII, information to downstream providers | SBOM; the due diligence record; the Annex XII pack. An SBOM can name the model and its lineage ([G7 SBOM for AI minimum elements](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/KI/SBOM-for-AI_minimum-elements.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=4)), but the CRA sets no expectation beyond top-level dependencies, and the formats cannot yet declare which dependency classes an SBOM covers, so a missing model entry is uninformative | The downstream provider, for Annex XII. The MSA, for the SBOM as far as it goes: it cannot tell a complete SBOM from one that never looked for models, because CycloneDX ([#1159](https://github.com/CycloneDX/specification/issues/1159)) and SPDX 3 ([#1105](https://github.com/spdx/spdx-3-model/issues/1105)) have no way yet to say which dependency classes were covered |
| Vulnerability and incident reporting | Art 14, actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents to CSIRT and ENISA; Annex I Part II (2) and (4) | Art 73, serious incident to the MSA within 15 days; Art 55(1)(c) for GPAI with systemic risk, to the AI Office | The notification; the vulnerability handling record | The receiving authority, from what it is told. Neither statute requires the report to carry anything the authority can re-derive |
| Documentation of what the thing is | Art 31 and Annex VII; retained 10 years or the support period under Art 13(13) | Art 11 and Annex IV for high-risk; Art 53(1)(a) and Annex XI for GPAI models | Technical documentation. AI Act Art 11(2) allows a single set where the PDE is also high-risk | Notified body, MSA, AI Office |
| Open source | Art 13(5) covers FOSS components not made available in the course of a commercial activity; Art 24(1) requires a steward to document a cybersecurity policy "in a verifiable manner" | Art 2(12) excludes AI systems under free and open-source licences unless high-risk or caught by Art 5 or 50; Art 53(2) exempts open-weight GPAI providers from 53(1)(a) and (b), except with systemic risk | The steward's documented policy. From the exempt open-weight provider, only the copyright policy and the public training-content summary of Art 53(1)(c) and (d) | MSA on reasoned request, for the steward. For the open-weight model's technical documentation, nobody; the AI Office can check only the two Art 53(1)(c) and (d) items. So an integrator has more it can demand of an open source library than of the model it embeds |

Two rows have no independent checker. AI Act logs are disclosed "to the extent such logs are under their control", and nothing asks the holder to show that the logs are complete and unaltered. An open-weight model exempt under Article 53(2) owes its integrator less than an open source library owes under [[Article 24]].
